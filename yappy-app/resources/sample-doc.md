# The Lighthouse Keeper

*A short tale, told in five parts — and a good way to see how Yappy reads structure.*

On the edge of the world, where the grey sea met the greyer sky, stood a lighthouse made of white stone. For forty years it had thrown its beam across the water, and for forty years the keeper had climbed its stairs each night.

> "A light is only worth keeping," he would say, "if there is someone out there to see it."

## A Quiet Routine

The keeper's days were simple. At dawn he polished the great lens until it shone. At dusk he lit the lamp and watched the first ships turn toward the harbour, guided by his light.

He kept a small garden in the lee of the tower — hardy things, mostly:

- thyme, for the wind never bothered it
- sea-kale, which seemed to like the salt
- a single stubborn rose that bloomed against all reason

### The Lens

The lens itself was his proudest charge. Cut from a hundred wedges of glass, it gathered the small flame at its heart and hurled it twenty miles out to sea. He treated it the way a musician treats an instrument.

## The Storm

One autumn night the storm came without warning. The wind tore at the shutters and the rain fell sideways. Out beyond the rocks, a fishing boat fought the swell, its tiny lantern blinking like a frightened star.

The keeper trimmed the wick, threw open the gallery door, and held the beam steady through the worst of it — until, near morning, the little boat slipped safely past the rocks and into calm water.

### What the Crew Saw

The fishermen later swore the light had seemed to *lean* toward them, as if the tower itself were reaching out a hand. The keeper only smiled when he heard this. A light does not lean. But a keeper can.

---

## Afterward

They say the fishermen never knew his name. But every year, on the anniversary of that night, a basket of fruit and fresh bread appeared at the foot of the tower, left by hands that remembered.

And the rose, somehow, kept blooming.
